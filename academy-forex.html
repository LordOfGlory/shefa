01 · What financial markets are

A market is a place where people agree a price. In forex, the “place” is a network of banks, firms and platforms. You exchange one currency for another. The quote you see is the last agreed rate, not a promise of the next one.

Currencies move because of interest rates, inflation, political risk, and how much of each currency people want to hold. You do not need a forecast to understand that. You need a description: what is being bought, in what size, and at what cost to you including spread.

If you cannot explain the pair you are looking at in one sentence — for example “US dollars versus Ugandan shilling” or “euro versus US dollar” — you are not ready to place an order. Naming the market is the first skill.

02 · Why a written plan comes first

A plan is a document you can read when you are impatient. It states which markets you will look at, when you will do nothing, how large a position may be, and how you will review the week. If it only lives in your head, it will change the moment price moves.

Write four lines before you ever use real money: (1) what you trade, (2) the maximum you will lose on one idea, (3) the hours you will sit at the screen, (4) the rule that stops you after a losing streak.

If you will not write the plan, do not pay for a course. The course cannot do that work for you.

03 · Reading a chart without folklore

A chart is a history of traded prices. Time on one axis, price on the other. Candles and bars pack open, high, low and close into one shape. They are not signals by themselves.

Support and resistance are observations: price has paused or reversed near a level before. That does not mean it will again. If you cannot point to swing highs and lows on the screen, you do not have a trend — you have a hope.

Practice this: hide every overlay, name the last swing high and low, and say whether the market is moving, ranging, or unclear. Unclear is an allowed answer. Trading unclear is not.

04 · Risk is the lesson that matters

Leverage multiplies both the gain and the loss. A small account can be reduced in a few decisions. Education does not change the arithmetic. If you cannot state, in money, how much one idea is allowed to cost you, you are guessing.

A practical rule: risk a small, fixed fraction of the account on one idea, and stop when that fraction is gone. Spreads, overnight costs and slippage are part of the cost.
